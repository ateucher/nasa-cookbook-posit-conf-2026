  • When you first think of NASA, you probably think of space, and rockets and moon rovers
  • Incredible, amazing, awe inspiring

  • What I now think of when I think of NASA - is:
  • looking back at Earth
  • using satellites to capture incredible data with different instruments so we can learn about our world

100+ petabytes of data

  • NASA’s Earth Science program manages over 100 petabytes of remote sensing data

  • contributes to scientific research all over the world,
  • helping us learn about climate change and its impacts, landscape and ocean biology, and water and atmospheric processes.
  • Really important things for the future of the world
  • Scientists need to get it, understand it, use it
  • Getting this data into the hands of scientists is a complicated task that is the primary responsibility of NASA staff across 11 different data centres.

  • This task has changed over the last few years
  • Like so many organisations, moved data from on-premesis servers to the cloud
  • Many advantages
  • Access patterns to the data have changed
  • Important that researchers can still easily access the data
  • Data centre staff have been challenged to both learn and teach new and more efficient ways of accessing and working with cloud-based satellite data.

NASA Openscapes Mentors

  • Working with NASA since 2021 to support Earth Science Data Center staff in their migration to the cloud
  • Data Center staff teach workshops on cloud data access, and from these produce amazing tutorials
  • for teaching scientists modern data science workflows
  • combining best practices for cloud data access, analysis, and scientific context
  • a critical resource for users, BUT
  • Data Centers were doing this in silos
    • duplicated effort
    • Not learning from each other
  • Openscapes has been working with mentors to build a framework for them to collaborate to create learning resources for their teams and communities.

Collaborative coworking is a force multiplier

  • We are in a Quarto session, and I want to emphasize how critical Quarto has been
  • But I also want to make sure to center the Mentors at NASA, and their passion, expertise, and leadership.
  • Talk about the collaborative coworking that has been a critical part of the success of the cookbook, and all of the other work mentors are doing to support their teams and communities.

What is coworking?

  • Biweekly hour and a half session, on Zoom, with NASA Mentors
  • Openscapes provides scaffolding by scheduling, creating shared notes docs
  • come together to work on the cookbook
  • I lead these sessions, looking out for what they need
  • Discuss what needs to be done / what we want to work on
  • Match up expertise / interest with needs
  • Breakout rooms to get things done
  • Design and create new content
  • Create issues, Review PRs
  • Onboard new contributors
  • Screenshare to get feedback or solve a problem together.
  • Seeing how other people work is a big part of transition to working more openly and collaboratively.
  • We (Openscapes) put time into the quarto scaffolding side, maintenance…
  • Having a tool that doesn’t require too much effort to use/learn if you have a different technical background makes coworking easier
